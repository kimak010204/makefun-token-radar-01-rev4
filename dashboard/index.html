How the implied value is worked out

implied = TVL × category multiple × growth × maturity × chains

  • Category multiple: the median of market-cap / TVL over the live tokens of the protocol's own category, shrunk toward the global median — the global prior counts as 3 live tokens, so one live token moves the multiple a quarter of the way from the global median toward its own ratio, and fifteen move it five-sixths of the way. Clamped to 0.05 – 1.5. The median, not the mean: one token with a tiny TVL and a huge market cap must not set the multiple for a whole category.
  • Growth: 1 + clamp(7-day TVL change, −50%, +150%) / 100 × 0.6. Seven days is noisy, so its weight is deliberately below one: momentum moves the value, it never decides it.
  • Maturity: 0.6 + min(age in days, 1095) / 1095 × 0.4 — older and still pre-token counts for more; unknown age counts as 365 days.
  • Chains: 1 + 0.02 × min(chains, 5).

Why shrink instead of cutting off: a category with only one or two live tokens still has evidence, and throwing it away would be the bigger error. The top three launches are Risk Curators, a category with exactly one live token — Api3, trading at 2.975 times TVL — and pricing all 38 pre-token curators at the global median as if that peer did not exist would understate them fourfold. Shrinking gives that peer a quarter of the say and the global median three quarters, landing the multiple at 0.904. The calibration table below shows the raw median, the live count, the weight, and the final multiple for every category, so a reader can see the blend — and every row carries a confidence label: priced (3+ live tokens), thin (1–2), or fallback (none).

What the model does not do: it does not predict whether or when a token is issued. A protocol's implied value is what its token would be worth if its record stayed as it is. A protocol can sit at the top of this board for years and never launch, and a small one can launch tomorrow — the board ranks value, not likelihood.
